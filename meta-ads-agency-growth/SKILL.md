# Meta Ads Agency Growth

Help an agency use Meta Ads to pursue qualified, attended sales conversations and profitable clients. Use the [agency workflow](references/agency-workflow.md) for intake, metrics, diagnosis, and deliverables, [acquisition system](references/acquisition-system.md) for the full funnel, creative tests, and proof review, and [Meta lead-generation guidance](references/meta-lead-generation.md) when designing or reviewing lead campaigns. The skill improves decision quality; it cannot guarantee appointments, clients, or revenue. This is an independent community-maintained skill and is not affiliated with or endorsed by Meta.

## Learn from videos when requested

When the user provides or identifies a video and asks to learn from it, use an available video-watching skill or tool to inspect the actual video. If no supported review method is available after considering the fallback below, explain the limitation and ask for a transcript or accessible media file; do not infer the video's advice from its title, thumbnail, or marketing page. Record the source and date, summarize only relevant tactics, and distinguish what the speaker explicitly says from your interpretation. Treat examples, results, and recommendations as hypotheses to assess against the agency's offer, funnel, economics, account data, current Meta controls, and policy. Carry forward only generalizable, evidence-compatible guidance. Do not copy scripts, proprietary frameworks, or identity. Do not edit this skill or repository unless the user asks for an update.

If a paid video-analysis tool is unavailable or out of credits, consider an open-source fallback when the environment supports it: use yt-dlp to retrieve available captions first; if captions are absent and the user has authorized media retrieval, use a local speech-to-text model such as faster-whisper or Whisper and inspect representative frames with a local video tool. Audio transcription alone does not establish what the video shows. Do not bypass sign-in, privacy, DRM, or other access controls. If the required tools or authorized media are unavailable, say exactly which evidence is missing and request the least burdensome input.

## Start with the actual job

Identify whether the user needs a launch plan, offer or creative, funnel review, live-account audit, optimization, or reporting. Establish whether the ads promote the agency's own services or a client's Meta Ads service. Keep account and client data separate. If the request concerns a client's ads, use that client's objective and economics, not the agency's acquisition targets. If the requested outcome is qualified appointments, optimize the work toward qualified appointments that actually book and show; do not equate form fills, messages, or cheap leads with success.

Ask only for missing inputs that change the decision: the offer and ideal client, geography, budget and maximum acceptable loss, target client value and gross margin, proof available for claims, sales capacity, account history, funnel and CRM stages, and the reporting period, currency, timezone, and attribution settings. When access is unavailable, use supplied exports or build a clearly labeled plan from assumptions. Do not imply that installing this skill grants Meta account access.

## Work through the acquisition system

1. **Offer first.** Before writing ads, establish exactly what the agency will offer: target client, service and scope, deliverables, price or terms, outcome the agency can credibly pursue, eligibility and exclusions, proof, capacity, and the next step. Research what the target market wants, has already tried, and needs explained to understand why this service is a credible fit. An offer is the actual service package and commercial terms, not just an ad hook, discount, or promise. If these are unclear or unsupported, help develop and validate the offer first; do not invent terms or launch ads around an unapproved offer.
2. **Economics and fit.** Define a qualified prospect, qualified attended call, client, and viable acquisition cost from the agency's margins and expected retention. Check whether the proposed volume can be served and sold, and whether the offer economics support paid acquisition.
3. **Creative.** Once the offer is clear and approved, plan both image ads and video ads for each campaign. Draft distinct message angles, not superficial rewrites. Connect each angle to the prospect's desire, past frustration, differentiating mechanism, and proof. Use the creative and copy to help the right prospect recognize fit and to set accurate expectations; avoid broad promises that attract people the agency cannot serve. Use specific, supportable explanations for why prior approaches may have failed and how the offer differs; do not invent causes or imply that an unproven mechanism is certain to work. Adapt each concept to its format: make the image understandable at a glance, and make the video's opening, captions, pacing, and call to action work without relying on sound. Create a test matrix with the hypothesis, audience, format, asset, landing path, and success signal. If a format cannot be produced, identify the missing asset and propose how to create it. Where practical, compare image and video while holding the audience, offer, and campaign objective steady; judge by qualified booked and attended calls, not clicks or views alone. Use original concepts and approved evidence; do not copy another advertiser's scripts or identity.
4. **Funnel and qualification.** Trace ad → opt-in or landing page → explanation or video sales letter (VSL), if justified → qualification gate → booking → confirmation and reminders → attendance → sale → cash collected. Define qualification with the client before launch, using observable service-fit and buying-readiness criteria. Configure the flow so only prospects who meet those criteria can access the booking calendar; route others to an appropriate alternative or respectful exit without a booking invitation. Before booking, give cold prospects enough information to make an informed decision: what the service includes, how it works, who it is for, relevant proof, and a realistic price range or terms when approved. Keep the landing page consistent with the ad, answer the questions the ad raises, and clearly explain what the booked call is for. A VSL is optional: test one when it helps explain or qualify a complex or high-consideration offer and enough qualified traffic exists to measure downstream outcomes. Prefer a direct page or booking flow when the offer is already clear, the video adds friction, or traffic is too limited to learn. After booking, use a clear thank-you page and a proportionate confirmation, reminder, and pre-call information sequence; test its effect on confirmed bookings, attendance, cancellations, and close rate. Do not automatically cancel a qualified booking for lack of confirmation unless that requirement was clearly disclosed and operationally appropriate. Check message consistency, form friction, mobile experience, calendar capacity, follow-up, and respectful handling of people who are not a fit. Compare qualified booking, attendance, close rate, and contribution—not video views alone—and account for sales-cycle lag.
5. **Measurement.** Verify the selected Meta optimization event matches a real, consistently recorded action. Assess every lead against the agreed qualification criteria and record the result in the CRM. For CRM feedback, send Meta only the agreed qualified-appointment outcome after a lead passes qualification and books; do not send raw or unqualified CRM leads as the quality signal. This gives Meta a more relevant signal about the prospects the agency values and may improve optimization over time, but it does not guarantee future lead quality. The ad platform may still record its own native form submission. If the account cannot use the qualified-appointment signal or the signal volume is too sparse for meaningful optimization, state that limitation and propose a bounded test or pause; do not substitute raw leads while claiming to optimize for appointment quality. Reconcile Meta, site, calendar, and CRM counts, and verify privacy, consent, matching, and deduplication before trusting results.
6. **Campaigns and optimization.** Treat broad targeting, campaign budget automation, campaign organization by creative concept, and creative testing volume as account-specific hypotheses. Consider open targeting only when geography and eligibility are correctly set, the qualified-appointment event is reliable, and enough valid outcomes are recorded to evaluate quality. Set a sustainable creative testing cadence from budget, spend distribution, production capacity, and the volume needed to learn; do not prescribe a universal number of new ads per week or assume each creative needs its own campaign. Diagnose creative fatigue from sustained changes in qualified-booking and attendance cost or quality, alongside delivery and engagement evidence; a creative's age or a fixed refresh schedule alone is not enough to call it fatigued. Choose settings based on signal maturity and current Meta features. Diagnose the weakest verified stage of the funnel before proposing a change. Prefer controlled, reversible tests with a budget cap and a review point.
7. **Agency delivery.** When the user asks about growing a Meta Ads service, connect acquisition promises to a repeatable delivery process: scope, owners, creative supply, reporting cadence, escalation, and what happens when results slow. In the weekly review, test, measure, and refine the weakest evidenced stage across ad response, qualification, attendance, and sales. Do not expand into unrelated agency management.

## Evidence and action boundaries

- Separate observed data, outside claims, your inference, and a proposed test. A case study or result from another advertiser is not a forecast for this agency.
- Report qualified attended-call cost and client acquisition cost when the underlying counts are available. Cheap leads or booked calls alone do not prove growth. Account for conversion lag, refunds, gross margin, and attribution limits.
- Do not apply universal creative counts, audience sizes, age limits, budget changes, or pause rules from another account. Do not reject a lead solely because of an email domain or other weak proxy.
- Check current Meta product controls, advertising rules, and local privacy requirements before a launch or event-collection change; videos and interfaces age.
- Draft changes first. Apply live account, funnel, CRM, or budget changes only when the user has authorized that action for the identified account and the available tools permit it. Confirm the spend cap and verify the result after applying.

## Protect the outcome

Before recommending spend, check that the client has a specific offer and viable unit economics, a defensible definition of a qualified appointment, capacity to respond and serve new clients, a functioning booking path, and a way to record lead outcomes. If a critical dependency is missing, name it and recommend a bounded validation step or fix before scaling. Never promise a guaranteed number of leads, appointments, clients, or revenue.

For a new campaign, produce a go/no-go checklist, a test plan with a user-approved budget cap, the primary business metric, a review point tied to data volume and sales-cycle lag, and clear conditions to pause or continue. Use client economics to derive a target cost per qualified attended call from an acceptable acquisition cost and the observed close rate. If close-rate or margin evidence is missing, show a scenario range and label assumptions; do not invent a benchmark. Stop or pause if tracking is unreliable, leads are not reaching the team, consent or policy requirements are unresolved, the budget cap is hit, or sales/delivery capacity is exceeded. When early results are weak, diagnose the earliest evidenced funnel constraint before changing several campaign variables at once.

## Return a useful deliverable

For a strategy: give the offer, target prospect, funnel, event plan, creative angles, test budget, and decision points. For an audit: give an evidence table, funnel metrics, root-cause hypotheses, and a ranked action list. For each action state the evidence, expected mechanism, effort, risk, owner, success metric, and review date or condition. Mark missing evidence and assumptions plainly. Attribute any outside source used and never present another advertiser's results as expected user results.
