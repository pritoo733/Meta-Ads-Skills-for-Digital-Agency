# Assess an agency offer before Meta Ads

Use this when an agency asks whether its offer is good, when an account audit begins, or before writing ads. A connected Meta account may show how an offer is advertised and how prior campaigns performed. Ask for the actual proposal, page, pricing, delivery scope, sales notes, and first-party outcomes before judging the commercial offer. Do not treat a catchy hook or another advertiser's result as validation.

## Reconstruct the offer

Write one plain-language line: **For [specific buyer with a specific problem], we deliver [service and scope] to pursue [buyer-valued result] through [real delivery method], for [price and terms], subject to [material eligibility and client responsibilities].** Mark every missing field. Keep the buyer's desired result distinct from the service mechanism and from the ad's opening line.

| Check | Evidence to request | Decision it changes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Buyer and urgency | Recent buyer interviews, sales-call notes, lost reasons, current alternatives, and who controls the budget. | Whether this segment wants the result now and is reachable through the planned Meta funnel. |
| Valuable outcome | The metric buyers name, why it matters, and the later business result it should lead to. | Whether the offer speaks to qualified appointments or clients rather than traffic or form fills alone. |
| Service and effort | Exact deliverables, setup time, client inputs, ownership, capacity, and realistic time to first value. | Whether the agency can fulfill the offer and whether required client work is clear. |
| Fit and quantity | The buyer's current scale, serviceable geography, ad budget, sales capacity, and evidence behind any proposed volume or timeline. | Whether a numerical claim fits this segment and whether qualification criteria match delivery reality. |
| Difference and proof | Real differences in service, process, scope, or evidence; comparable case studies with dates, denominator, outcome stage, and permission. | Whether the offer can be distinguished truthfully without inventing a mechanism or presenting an exceptional case as typical. |
| Price and margin | Fees, ad spend, setup and sales costs, delivery cost, refund terms, collected cash, acceptable CAC, and payback. | Whether paid acquisition can work for the agency and whether clients can reasonably fund the service. |
| Risk terms | Guarantee or trial conditions, exclusions, remedy, agency exposure, and operational owner, if offered. | Whether terms are feasible, clearly disclosed before booking, and supported by evidence. A guarantee is optional. |

Segment by actual buyer needs, capacity, and problem. Do not assume the largest buyer or a creator's revenue threshold is the best market for every agency. A higher promised volume is useful only when the agency can deliver it at the proposed budget and the buyer wants it. If a smaller package serves another eligible segment, present it as a clear alternative with its own terms; do not attract people with an undisclosed promise that applies only to a different segment.

Ask what prospects tried before and why they doubt this service. Explain a genuine difference that the agency can show in its workflow or results. If the service is Meta Ads, make that clear before the booking decision even when the ad leads with the buyer's desired outcome. Preserve the same offer and material conditions across ad, page, qualification form, VSL if used, sales call, and agreement.

Diagnose the offer, its explanation, and the funnel as separate possible constraints. If buyers want the service but misunderstand the ad or page, revise the explanation before replacing the package. If qualified people understand the package but decline its price or terms, investigate value, proof, economics, and fit. Improve one working Meta funnel before adding another unless evidence shows a distinct segment needs a separate path.

## Give a verdict with a next test

- **Ready for a capped test:** buyer, problem, service, terms, fit criteria, and economics are clear; claims are supported; capacity and the qualification/booking path can be verified. State what the pilot must still prove.
- **Revise before ads:** a specific defect would attract the wrong prospects or make delivery uneconomic—for example, unclear scope, an unsupported outcome claim, hidden spend requirement, weak fit criteria, or a promise the agency cannot serve. Show the smallest correction and its owner.
- **Insufficient evidence:** critical facts are missing. Name the missing evidence and the quickest appropriate validation step, such as buyer interviews, proposal review, sales-call analysis, a page test, or a small capped Meta pilot once the launch gates pass.

Give each verdict an evidence table: **criterion → observation → source and date → confidence → action**. Do not reduce it to an arbitrary numeric score or a probability of success. If prior campaigns exist, compare the actual offer and cohort: impressions and response, lead qualification reasons, booked and attended appointments, proposal acceptance, clients, collected revenue, and margin. A high click rate with weak qualification suggests a message or fit problem; attended calls that rarely buy require review of offer, price, proof, sales handling, and delivery. Diagnose rather than assuming a single cause.

Before launching a revised offer, record a testable hypothesis, the unchanged elements, approved spend cap, primary qualified appointment outcome, downstream economics, and review condition. Compare offer versions with comparable audience, creative exposure, and mature cohort data when possible. If several elements change, label the result exploratory. Do not use cold DM or email outreach tests as evidence that a Meta ad offer works.

## Public-video research used for this guidance

English captions were reviewed on 2026-10-11; player visuals and the speakers' case-study numbers were not independently verified. These videos informed hypotheses, not performance benchmarks:

- [Offer framework, 2023](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tN7fHy3ls0Q): buyer-valued metric, segment-specific volume, and feasibility of conditions. The recommendation to reveal restrictive terms only on a call was rejected.
- [Offer and market desire, 2022](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OPoGRcWL1Ao): desired outcome and credible difference in a crowded market. The suggestion to obscure the actual delivery channel was rejected.
- [Offer and delivery model, 2022](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jMvxb54obb0): client effort and realistic time to value affect the appeal of a service offer.
- [Offer, message, and funnel discussion, 2022](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DzWhrRuhGkA): diagnose the actual package separately from its copy and delivery path; research buyer objections before changing all three.
- [Offer example, 2025](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GncXkPqrAoc): interviews, distinct positioning, and proof matched to the claim; large numbers were treated as unverified promotional examples.
- [Paid-ad funnel example, 2025](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Abrp1zPQrlo): offer-to-ad continuity, proof, and qualification before booking; universal budget and creative counts were rejected.
- [Live agency discussion, 2025](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qZofo-l28TM): buyer segments can want different outcomes; check client delivery fit and unit economics rather than taking every booked lead.

The search page also contains cold outreach, older videos, Shorts, and repeated listings. Do not carry those channels or methods into this Meta Ads skill.
