# Build an agency offer for Meta Ads acquisition

Use this when an agency has no clear package, wants to change its package, or asks what to offer to win clients through Meta Ads. Build options from what the agency can actually deliver. A sample package is a draft, never a proven result for a new agency or niche. After building options, use [offer assessment](offer-assessment.md) to decide whether the selected offer is ready for a capped test.

## Gather only decision-changing inputs

Ask for the agency's actual services and delivery method; niches it knows and can serve; geography; buyer problem and desired business outcome; existing client results and permissions; team capacity; sales process; fees, delivery costs, client ad-spend requirements, and acceptable acquisition cost. Request recent prospect or client language, won and lost proposals, objections, and reasons buyers chose alternatives. If an input is missing, label it unknown and propose a way to learn it. Do not invent a niche, price, guarantee, result, testimonial, or case study.

Prefer a segment where the agency has relevant proof, can solve an urgent problem, can reach eligible buyers through Meta, and can fulfill the work profitably. If several segments fit, keep them distinct rather than averaging their needs into a generic “business owners” offer. A client's willingness and ability to fund media, respond to leads, and close sales also affect fit.

## Construct two or three plausible options

For each option, specify:

| Component | What to write |
| --- | --- |
| Buyer and trigger | Specific business type, size or stage where relevant, problem, and why the buyer would act now. |
| Outcome | Buyer-valued result to pursue, with a measurable stage such as qualified booked appointments; distinguish it from attended calls and closed clients. Do not promise a number without evidence. |
| Delivery | Actual Meta Ads service, image and video creative responsibility, funnel and qualification, calendar, CRM feedback, follow-up ownership, reporting, and what is outside scope. |
| Commercial terms | Setup and recurring fees or another sustainable model, media spend paid by whom, contract length, cancellation or refund terms, and client responsibilities. Mark unknown numbers for approval. |
| Fit and proof | Eligibility and exclusions, examples the agency may truthfully use, and which claims remain unproven. |
| Capacity and economics | Onboarding and ongoing effort, available slots, gross contribution, plausible acquisition-cost ceiling, and risks if lead volume or attendance changes. |

Make options materially different where the agency can support them. Examples include a managed Meta Ads and qualified-booking service for a defined niche; a limited setup plus handover package for a team that will run its own ads; or a creative-and-funnel improvement package for an agency with proven production but no capacity to own lead follow-up. These are service shapes, not default recommendations. Do not imply every agency can provide all parts or that merely adding a guarantee makes an offer stronger.

In a crowded category, start with the outcome buyers already ask for, then identify a verifiable difference in the delivery method, scope, speed, or effort required from the client. Explain why that difference could help this segment, and check that the agency can demonstrate it. The ad may lead with the desired outcome, but identify the actual Meta Ads service and material terms before the prospect books; do not imply that a different channel or undisclosed guarantee is included.

Write each option as a clear, conditional offer statement: **For [buyer], we [deliverables and method] to pursue [buyer-valued outcome], with [price and material terms], for clients who [fit and responsibilities].** Separate the service package from ad hooks and landing-page copy. Keep terms visible before a prospect books, and carry the same offer through ad, page, qualification, call, and agreement.

## Compare and choose

Compare options side by side on buyer demand, evidence, ability to deliver, differentiation buyers can understand, price and margin, reachability through Meta, and sales and onboarding capacity. Cite the source and date of each observation, note contrary evidence, and identify unknowns. Recommend one option to validate first, or state that none is ready. Explain why this choice fits the agency rather than presenting a universal best package. If a client has already selected a service or niche, work within that choice unless the evidence shows a material problem.

Give the agency a concrete output: the recommended offer statement; scope and client responsibilities; proposed pricing structure with any unknown amounts clearly blank; eligibility and qualification criteria; allowable proof and claims; two alternative packages with their tradeoffs when useful; a list of assumptions requiring approval; and the smallest next validation step. Do not silently approve draft terms on the agency's behalf.

## Validate before spending, then learn from the funnel

Use buyer interviews, sales-call notes, proposals, first-party results, and delivery costs to test demand and feasibility. If sufficient evidence exists, propose a capped Meta pilot with a stated hypothesis, audience, offer, image and video angles, approved spend limit, qualified-booking metric, downstream attendance and client economics, and a review condition. Do not use cold outreach response or another advertiser's case study as proof that this agency's Meta offer works.

After the pilot, inspect the earliest evidenced constraint: low relevant response may indicate buyer, message, or offer mismatch; many leads but few qualified bookings point to fit or funnel problems; attended calls without sales call for investigation of the offer, price, proof, sales process, and service. Change the diagnosed element and retest. See [validation and decisions](validation-and-decisions.md) for the economic and cohort rules. A good offer is a testable hypothesis until this agency's buyers and results support it.
