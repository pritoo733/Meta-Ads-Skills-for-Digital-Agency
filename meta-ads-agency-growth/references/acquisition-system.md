# Agency acquisition system

Use this framework to review the complete path from Meta ad spend to profitable agency clients. Treat each stage as a place to gather evidence, not as a fixed funnel template.

## Define the result before buying traffic

Write down the agency's ideal client profile, service area, minimum fit criteria, offer, proof, available sales and delivery capacity, and the disqualifiers. Define a **qualified appointment** as a meeting that is booked by a reachable prospect who meets the agreed criteria; report attendance as a separate downstream result. Have the client approve the criteria and the data recorded for each one. Do not use protected traits or sensitive personal data as qualification shortcuts.

Check whether the service has enough addressable demand on Meta and whether the likely sales value can support paid acquisition. Derive a target media cost per qualified attended call from the maximum acceptable media CAC multiplied by the observed close rate from qualified attended calls. If either value is unknown, model scenarios and mark them as assumptions. Establish a maximum test loss the client can afford before launch.

Estimate the serviceable market from the actual client criteria, geography, language, and buyer count. Aim for a segment specific enough to support relevant messaging and broad enough to sustain the planned test and delivery capacity. Do not adopt a creator's fixed audience-size threshold as a universal rule, and do not equate a platform's estimated reach with the number of genuine buyers. If the market may be too small or demand is unclear, validate the estimate and acquisition path before forecasting scale.

## Build the operating path

Before launch, test the whole path as a prospect: ad promise → form or page and CRM capture → qualification gate → booking calendar → confirmed booking and qualified-booked Meta feedback → thank-you page, owner notification, and reminders → appointment outcome. Keep the ad and landing page congruent, answer the questions or expectations raised by the ad, and make the purpose of the call clear. Use conditional form logic or an equivalent server-side gate so only prospects who meet the client-approved criteria see the calendar. Route ineligible prospects to a suitable alternative, an optional nurture path with appropriate consent, or a polite end screen; keep these paths separate from the qualified-appointment signal. Verify that changing answers, duplicate submissions, or incomplete forms cannot accidentally bypass qualification or create a qualified-booking event. Confirm mobile usability, tracking and deduplication, privacy notice and consent, time zone, calendar slots, and backup handling. Assign who responds to leads and what response-time target the client can actually meet. Use the thank-you page and follow-up to set expectations, provide useful pre-call information, and make confirmation easy. Test confirmation and reminder steps against confirmed bookings, attendance, cancellations, and close rate. If a confirmation requirement can cancel a booking, disclose it before booking and check that it is operationally appropriate. Verify qualified and unqualified test journeys arrive and are recorded correctly before media spend begins.

Choose the conversion route to match the offer and operating capacity. In-account lead forms can reduce friction; use qualifying questions sparingly and set expectations clearly. A website page or VSL can explain a complex, high-consideration service and filter for fit, but it adds steps and can reduce volume. Messaging can work when someone is available to respond and qualify promptly. Treat each step and content asset as a hypothesis; compare cost per qualified booked and attended call, not raw lead cost alone. Keep any hook or curiosity in the ad truthful and resolve the expectation it creates on the next page; do not create misleading open loops or hide material terms to force a booking.

Connect the CRM when feasible and lawful. Record lead source, qualification status, booking, attendance, and sale consistently. For CRM-to-Meta optimization feedback, send only the agreed qualified-and-booked appointment outcome when the supported setup allows it; keep raw and unqualified leads in the CRM and do not use them as the appointment-quality signal. If account features or event volume cannot support this signal, state the limitation and recommend a bounded test or delay/pause rather than silently substituting raw leads. Never send an event before the outcome occurred, fabricate qualification, or assume CRM integration guarantees quality. Follow current platform requirements and data-protection law.

## Creative and audience testing

Creative is an important input, but do not treat it as the only determinant of performance. Offer fit, delivery, landing-page experience, qualification, follow-up, sales handling, tracking, and budget also affect outcomes. Develop distinct concepts and format variations at a pace the budget and production team can sustain. There is no universal weekly creative count. Give each test enough spend and time to produce interpretable evidence; avoid splitting a small budget across so many ads or campaigns that none can learn. Organize campaigns by concept or format only when that structure helps answer a clear question and the budget can support it.

Build each concept around one prospect, one problem or desired outcome, one credible explanation of the offer's difference, and one clear next step. A strong opening can earn attention, but it must lead into the offer the ad actually promotes. Explain past failures only when the explanation is supported; do not invent a mechanism or imply that a new process guarantees success. Make image ads understandable at a glance and make video ads work with sound off through clear on-screen text and a direct opening. For Reels, check the current placement specifications and safe zones; test vertical 9:16 video with audio while retaining suitable image executions. [Meta's Reels guidance](https://www.facebook.com/business/ads/facebook-instagram-reels-ads) and reported format tests can inform a hypothesis, but do not treat those averages as a forecast for another account. Test materially different angles and executions, not just cosmetic rewrites.

Match the appeal to the prospect's awareness and real alternatives. Use customer interviews, sales-call notes, CRM loss reasons, and relevant market research to learn what prospects have already tried and what they doubt. Treat unverified explanations of why alternatives failed as hypotheses. In the ad, state the service and its deliverables clearly even when the opening focuses on the buyer's desired outcome. A useful brief records: buyer and fit → desired outcome → what the agency actually does → supporting proof with context → clear call to action.

## Proof and claim review

Map each factual claim to evidence before publishing it. Use authentic case studies, testimonials, reviews, or performance records only when the agency has permission to use them and can explain what they show. Keep the client and measurement context, relevant time period, denominator or sample size, and source clear where material. Distinguish booked appointments from attended calls, pipeline from collected revenue, and correlation from demonstrated causation. A receipt, dashboard screenshot, or testimonial may support a specific statement; it does not by itself establish typical results, prove that advertising caused the result, or forecast another agency's outcome. Do not edit evidence in a way that changes its meaning, fabricate scarcity or risk reversal, or present an exceptional result as typical. Review claims against applicable advertising rules and the actual offer terms.

Use proof to answer the prospect's reasonable questions and reduce uncertainty, not to pressure someone into a decision. Explain what happens on the call, what the offer includes, and any material price or eligibility terms before booking when available. Keep testimonials and results consistent with the ad, page, and sales conversation; obtain approval before sharing client-identifying or confidential details.

Treat show-up improvements as experiments. Compare qualified-booking and attendance cohorts with consistent definitions, comparable lead sources, enough volume, and time for scheduled appointments to occur. Record changes to lead time, calendar availability, reminders, contact attempts, and confirmation steps so a change in show rate is not attributed to one tactic when several changed together. Keep bookings and attendance as separate outcomes, and disclose any confirmation requirement before a prospect books.

Treat open or broad audience settings as a test, not a default promise. They are most interpretable when the campaign's geography and eligibility are correct, the qualified-booking event fires only after the agreed gate, and CRM outcomes can be reconciled. Compare qualified booking and attendance quality across a mature cohort. If the event is unreliable or too sparse to evaluate, fix measurement or use a bounded audience test based on account evidence; do not assume that narrower targeting or broader targeting will solve the problem by itself.

Plan creative refreshes from evidence, not a fixed calendar or a universal fatigue window. Review delivery, reach and frequency where available, response, qualified bookings, attendance, and marginal cost against a relevant baseline. Allow for attribution and sales-cycle lag before concluding that a recent creative has weakened. Replace or expand concepts when sustained evidence supports it, while protecting tests from unnecessary budget fragmentation.

## Turn account walkthroughs into a real campaign test

Translate examples into the three Ads Manager levels, while choosing current settings from the account's actual goal and signal:

- **Campaign:** use the objective and conversion location that fit the intended qualified-booking journey. Use the deepest reliable optimization event the account supports. If CRM feedback volume or eligibility is insufficient, state that limitation and keep the test bounded; do not label raw leads as qualified appointments.
- **Ad set:** specify the eligible geography, audience hypothesis, placements, budget, and conversion event. Keep the initial structure simple enough for the approved budget to produce interpretable results. Add or separate ad sets only to answer a real question, such as distinct markets or materially different audience hypotheses.
- **Ad:** test materially different image and video concepts. Each should make the intended buyer, problem or desired result, actual service, supporting proof, terms, and next step understandable. Keep the primary text, creative, headline, CTA, and destination consistent. Use the ad to prequalify honestly; do not hide who the service is for or imply false scarcity, a different fee model, or guaranteed results.

One supplied campaign walkthrough described a lead campaign with one ad set, a main static image, and an instant form using business-fit questions; another described testing a larger group of image ads and sending form submitters to a booking path. These are speaker-reported account examples, not recommended default structures or performance benchmarks. A separate example's pay-per-result advertising language did not match the service's actual payment terms; reject that mismatch. Do not copy the ads or scripts. Rebuild the test using the agency's approved offer and gate the calendar until qualification is confirmed. Compare cost per qualified booked and attended appointment, not the reported cost per raw lead. ([single-ad-set example](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=umpKrYXoWeA); [image-ad testing example](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OJ7veFtDxaQ); [offer-mismatch example](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LxaDD7eW_9E))

For copy, write from the buyer's verified language, then check each line against the offer and destination:

1. **Opening:** name a relevant buyer problem or desired outcome; avoid bait-and-switch curiosity.
2. **Fit:** say who the service is for and use only qualification details that matter.
3. **Offer:** explain the actual service and how it works in plain language.
4. **Proof:** use approved evidence with context; omit unsupported numbers and guarantees.
5. **Terms and CTA:** disclose material conditions before booking and invite the prospect to the next real step.

Treat this as a review checklist, not a rigid copy template. Shorten or reorder it to fit the format while preserving clarity and truthful expectations.

## Four stages to inspect

1. **Ad response.** Check delivery, audience eligibility, creative relevance, clicks, and the match between the ad promise and the next step.
2. **Explanation and qualification.** Review the landing page or form, offer clarity, proof, fit criteria, friction, and how prospects understand the service before booking.
3. **Attendance.** Compare qualified bookings with attended calls. Check calendar availability, lead time, reminders, contactability, consent, and rescheduling.
4. **Sales and delivery.** Review qualified attended calls, proposals, wins, collected revenue, margin, onboarding capacity, client outcomes, and retention.

Find the earliest stage where reliable evidence shows a constraint. Do not change ads to solve a sales or delivery issue without evidence connecting the two.

## Measurement questions

- Are stage definitions consistent across the ad platform, website, calendar, and CRM?
- Are events recorded only when the corresponding action actually happens?
- Can the agency connect lead source and campaign to qualification, attendance, sale, and collected cash?
- Is the cohort old enough to account for the normal sales cycle and conversion lag?
- Are privacy notices, permissions, and consent appropriate before customer data is shared with an advertising platform?

Separate platform-attributed results from first-party CRM records. Explain missing, duplicated, delayed, or immature data before calculating rates. Evaluate client acquisition cost against contribution margin and a stated payback period.

Report the funnel with counts as well as rates: spend → leads → contactable → qualified → qualified booked → qualified attended → proposals → clients → collected revenue. Useful calculations include:

- Cost per qualified booked appointment = spend / qualified booked appointments.
- Cost per qualified attended appointment = spend / qualified attended appointments.
- Booking rate = qualified booked appointments / qualified leads.
- Show rate = qualified attended appointments / qualified booked appointments.
- Close rate = clients / qualified attended appointments.
- Media CAC = spend / clients; calculate fully loaded CAC separately when costs are available.

State the numerator, denominator, cohort dates, currency, attribution source, and lag for every rate. Do not disguise a small denominator with a high percentage or treat booked calls as attended calls.

## Weekly review

Compare the current period with a relevant baseline. Record stage counts, costs, meaningful changes, data-quality limits, and decisions. Choose one bounded test for the earliest evidenced constraint; name its owner, budget cap, success signal, and review point. Keep booked appointments separate from attended calls and closed revenue.

Use the agency's own evidence and delivery capacity to set targets. Examples from other advertisers can suggest questions, but they do not establish a forecast or a universal benchmark.

## Budget growth and reinvestment

If the agency wants to grow spend from revenue generated by ads, model the decision from collected cash and contribution, not attributed or contracted revenue alone. Account for fulfillment costs, sales costs, refunds, taxes or reserves where relevant, payment timing, and the cash needed to operate. Set a reinvestment amount only after the client approves a reserve and maximum loss.

Treat any scaling spreadsheet as a scenario, not a forecast. Recalculate with observed qualified-booking and attended-call costs, show rate, close rate, collected cash, and sales-cycle lag. Do not assume those rates or costs remain constant as spend rises. Increase budget in bounded steps only when a mature cohort meets the agreed economics and quality thresholds; monitor marginal cost and delivery capacity after each step.

## Test and stop decisions

Use one written hypothesis at a time where practical. Set the spend cap, audience and offer scope, primary metric, minimum evidence needed to decide, review date, and stop conditions before launch. Do not invent a universal spend threshold or promise a particular appointment count. Keep the test running long enough to account for the user's buying cycle only while tracking, lead handling, budget, policy status, and delivery capacity remain sound.

Pause immediately for broken lead delivery or tracking, unauthorized or noncompliant data use, policy/account restrictions, a breached spend cap, or an overwhelmed sales team. Use [validation and decisions](validation-and-decisions.md) to distinguish immature cohorts and insufficient evidence from a rejected hypothesis. Continue only within the existing approved cap when the remaining test can answer the question. At the cap or an agreed stop condition, pause further spend and investigate the earliest failure: delivery, click-to-lead, lead-to-contact, contact-to-qualification, qualification-to-booking, show rate, or close rate. Change the diagnosed constraint, document the rationale, and require a newly bounded test before increasing budget. If results meet the client's pre-agreed quality and economics targets across a mature cohort, scale gradually while checking marginal cost and service capacity.
