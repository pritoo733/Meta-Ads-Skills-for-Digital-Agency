# Agency acquisition system

Use this framework to review the complete path from Meta ad spend to profitable agency clients. Treat each stage as a place to gather evidence, not as a fixed funnel template.

## Define the result before buying traffic

Write down the agency's ideal client profile, service area, minimum fit criteria, offer, proof, available sales and delivery capacity, and the disqualifiers. Define a **qualified appointment** as a meeting that is booked by a reachable prospect who meets the agreed criteria; report attendance as a separate downstream result. Have the client approve the criteria and the data recorded for each one. Do not use protected traits or sensitive personal data as qualification shortcuts.

Check whether the service has enough addressable demand on Meta and whether the likely sales value can support paid acquisition. Derive a target media cost per qualified attended call from the maximum acceptable media CAC multiplied by the observed close rate from qualified attended calls. If either value is unknown, model scenarios and mark them as assumptions. Establish a maximum test loss the client can afford before launch.

## Build the operating path

Before launch, test the whole path as a prospect: ad promise → form or page → qualification → booking calendar → CRM record → owner notification → follow-up → appointment outcome. Confirm mobile usability, tracking and deduplication, privacy notice and consent, time zone, calendar slots, and backup handling. Assign who responds to leads and what response-time target the client can actually meet. Verify test leads arrive correctly before media spend begins.

Choose the conversion route to match the offer and operating capacity. In-account lead forms can reduce friction; use qualifying questions sparingly and set expectations clearly. A website page or VSL can explain a complex, high-consideration service and filter for fit, but it adds steps and can reduce volume. Messaging can work when someone is available to respond and qualify promptly. Treat these as hypotheses and compare cost per qualified booked and attended call, not raw lead cost alone.

Connect the CRM when feasible and lawful. Record lead source, qualification status, booking, attendance, and sale consistently. For CRM-to-Meta optimization feedback, send only the agreed qualified-and-booked appointment outcome when the supported setup allows it; keep raw and unqualified leads in the CRM and do not use them as the appointment-quality signal. If account features or event volume cannot support this signal, state the limitation and recommend a bounded test or delay/pause rather than silently substituting raw leads. Never send an event before the outcome occurred, fabricate qualification, or assume CRM integration guarantees quality. Follow current platform requirements and data-protection law.

## Four stages to inspect

1. **Ad response.** Check delivery, audience eligibility, creative relevance, clicks, and the match between the ad promise and the next step.
2. **Explanation and qualification.** Review the landing page or form, offer clarity, proof, fit criteria, friction, and how prospects understand the service before booking.
3. **Attendance.** Compare qualified bookings with attended calls. Check calendar availability, lead time, reminders, contactability, consent, and rescheduling.
4. **Sales and delivery.** Review qualified attended calls, proposals, wins, collected revenue, margin, onboarding capacity, client outcomes, and retention.

Find the earliest stage where reliable evidence shows a constraint. Do not change ads to solve a sales or delivery issue without evidence connecting the two.

## Measurement questions

- Are stage definitions consistent across the ad platform, website, calendar, and CRM?
- Are events recorded only when the corresponding action actually happens?
- Can the agency connect lead source and campaign to qualification, attendance, sale, and collected cash?
- Is the cohort old enough to account for the normal sales cycle and conversion lag?
- Are privacy notices, permissions, and consent appropriate before customer data is shared with an advertising platform?

Separate platform-attributed results from first-party CRM records. Explain missing, duplicated, delayed, or immature data before calculating rates. Evaluate client acquisition cost against contribution margin and a stated payback period.

Report the funnel with counts as well as rates: spend → leads → contactable → qualified → qualified booked → qualified attended → proposals → clients → collected revenue. Useful calculations include:

- Cost per qualified booked appointment = spend / qualified booked appointments.
- Cost per qualified attended appointment = spend / qualified attended appointments.
- Booking rate = qualified booked appointments / qualified leads.
- Show rate = qualified attended appointments / qualified booked appointments.
- Close rate = clients / qualified attended appointments.
- Media CAC = spend / clients; calculate fully loaded CAC separately when costs are available.

State the numerator, denominator, cohort dates, currency, attribution source, and lag for every rate. Do not disguise a small denominator with a high percentage or treat booked calls as attended calls.

## Weekly review

Compare the current period with a relevant baseline. Record stage counts, costs, meaningful changes, data-quality limits, and decisions. Choose one bounded test for the earliest evidenced constraint; name its owner, budget cap, success signal, and review point. Keep booked appointments separate from attended calls and closed revenue.

Use the agency's own evidence and delivery capacity to set targets. Examples from other advertisers can suggest questions, but they do not establish a forecast or a universal benchmark.

## Test and stop decisions

Use one written hypothesis at a time where practical. Set the spend cap, audience and offer scope, primary metric, minimum evidence needed to decide, review date, and stop conditions before launch. Do not invent a universal spend threshold or promise a particular appointment count. Keep the test running long enough to account for the user's buying cycle only while tracking, lead handling, budget, policy status, and delivery capacity remain sound.

Pause immediately for broken lead delivery or tracking, unauthorized or noncompliant data use, policy/account restrictions, a breached spend cap, or an overwhelmed sales team. At a planned review point, if there are no qualified bookings or unit economics are outside the approved tolerance, stop further spend and investigate the earliest failure: delivery, click-to-lead, lead-to-contact, contact-to-qualification, qualification-to-booking, show rate, or close rate. Change the diagnosed constraint, document the rationale, and require a newly bounded test before increasing budget. If results meet the client's pre-agreed quality and economics targets across a mature cohort, scale gradually while checking marginal cost and service capacity.
